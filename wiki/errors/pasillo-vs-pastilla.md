# pasillo vs pastilla — Aisle vs Pill

## What Goes Wrong

*Pasillo* (aisle/corridor) and *pastilla* (pill/tablet) look and sound similar. Learners swap them in supermarket or pharmacy contexts.

## Why It Happens

Both words share the *pa-s-ill-* sound pattern and end in -a. They appear in similar everyday situations (shopping, asking for directions in a store or pharmacy).

## Correct Usage

**pasillo** — aisle (supermarket), corridor, hallway
- *El aceite está en el pasillo tres.* — The oil is in aisle three.
- *Camina por el pasillo.* — Walk down the corridor.

**pastilla** — pill, tablet, lozenge; also a bar (of soap, chocolate)
- *Toma dos pastillas al día.* — Take two pills a day.
- *una pastilla de jabón* — a bar of soap

**Memory hook:** *pasillo* has two l's and no t → *pasillo* = passage/aisle. *Pastilla* has -till- in the middle → think "till you're well" (medicine).
