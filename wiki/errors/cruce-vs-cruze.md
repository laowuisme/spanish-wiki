## What Goes Wrong

Writing the usted command of `cruzar` as `Cruze` instead of `Cruce`.

## Why It Happens

`cruzar` looks like it should just add the regular command ending, and the intuitive spelling from the infinitive stem (`cruz-`) is `cruze`. But `-zar` verbs never keep the `z` before an `e` ending — the `z` becomes `c` in every form built on `-e` (present subjunctive, negative tú/vosotros commands, and all formal/usted commands), not just the preterite yo-form. See [[conjugation-spelling-patterns]] for the full rule across `-car`/`-gar`/`-zar` verbs.

## Correct Usage

`cruce` (usted command), `no cruces` (negative tú command), `crucé` (preterite yo) — the `z` never appears before `e` in this verb.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| Cruze la calle. | **Cruce la calle.** |
| No cruzes aquí. | (this one is actually correct — negative tú is `no cruces`, so the risk runs the other way too) |
