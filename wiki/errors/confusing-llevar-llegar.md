# confusing llevar / llegar

## What Goes Wrong

The words `llevar` and `llegar` look and sound similar but mean entirely different things. They are frequently mixed up:

- `llevar` — to carry, to wear, to take (someone/something somewhere), to contain
- `llegar` — to arrive

Example mistake: using `lleva` (carries/wears) when meaning `llega` (arrives), or vice versa.

## Why It Happens

Both verbs start with `ll-`, have similar length, and appear in common everyday contexts. The visual and auditory similarity is enough to trigger confusion, especially in reading comprehension or rapid speech.

Additionally, `llevar` has multiple meanings (carry, wear, contain, duration) making it harder to pin down — so when unsure, learners sometimes default to the wrong verb.

## Correct Usage

| Verb | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| `llegar` | `Llega a las ocho.` | He/she arrives at eight. |
| `llegar` | `¿Cuándo llegas?` | When do you arrive? |
| `llevar` | `Llevo una mochila.` | I'm carrying a backpack. |
| `llevar` | `Lleva un sombrero.` | He's wearing a hat. |
| `llevar` | `No llevan chiles.` | They don't include chiles. |
| `llevar` | `Llevo dos años aquí.` | I've been here two years. |

Memory trick: `llEGAR` = llEGAl into the country → **arrives**. `llEVAR` = llEVAte something → **carries/takes**.
