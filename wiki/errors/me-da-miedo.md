# me da miedo (the thing scares you)

## What Goes Wrong

The 2026-11-15 note lists `La sangre me da miedo` under Keep Forgetting. The note shows only the correct sentence. It does not show what you wrote or forgot.

## Why It Happens

Not known from the notes. The note does not say which part you forget. [[dar-expressions]] and [[tener-expressions]] show two different ways to say the feeling. In English the person is the subject ("I fear blood"). In Spanish with `dar`, the thing that scares you is the subject.

## Correct Usage

| Way to say it | Subject | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `dar miedo` | the thing that scares you | `La sangre me da miedo.` — Blood scares me. |
| `dar miedo` (plural subject) | the things that scare you | `Me dan miedo los truenos.` — Thunder scares me. |
| `tener miedo` | you | `Tengo miedo de la sangre.` — I am afraid of blood. |

- `me` is the indirect object. It shows who feels the fear.
- `da` agrees with the subject `la sangre`. Use `dan` for a plural subject.
- Memory hook: with `dar`, the thing "gives" you the fear. `La sangre` is the giver, so `da` follows `la sangre`.
