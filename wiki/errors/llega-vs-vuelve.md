# llega vs vuelve — Arrives vs Returns

## What Goes Wrong

`llega` (from `llegar`) and `vuelve` (from `volver`) both can translate as "comes back" or imply someone is now present, but they have different meanings. Learners use one when the other is correct.

## Why It Happens

In English "arrives" and "returns" can sometimes feel interchangeable in casual speech ("she's back"). In Spanish they are distinct:
- `llegar` = to arrive (reaching a destination for the first time, or simply arriving)
- `volver` = to return (coming back to a place you've been before)

The direction of the movement matters: `llegar` is neutral arrival; `volver` implies a return trip / going back.

## Correct Usage

| Verb | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| `llegar` | `Llegamos al aeropuerto.` | We arrived at the airport. |
| `llegar` | `¿Cuándo llega el tren?` | When does the train arrive? |
| `volver` | `Vuelvo a casa.` | I'm going back home. |
| `volver` | `¿Cuándo vuelves?` | When are you coming back? |
| `volver` | `Ella vuelve mañana.` | She's coming back tomorrow. |

Also in the infinitive context: `volver a + infinitive` = to do something again:
- `Vuelve a llamar.` — He calls again.
