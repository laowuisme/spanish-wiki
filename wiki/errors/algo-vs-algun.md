# algo vs algún — Something vs Some

## What Goes Wrong

`algo` and `algún/alguno/alguna` are both indefinite but function differently. Learners substitute one for the other.

## Why It Happens

Both relate to "some" or "something" in English, making them feel interchangeable. But:
- `algo` is a pronoun meaning **something** (invariable — no gender/number)
- `algún/alguno/alguna` is an adjective/pronoun meaning **some [noun]** (variable — must agree with noun)

## Correct Usage

**algo** — something (stands alone, no noun)
- `Quiero algo.` — I want something.
- `Tengo ganas de beber algo.` — I feel like drinking something.
- `¿Hay algo para comer?` — Is there something to eat?

**algún + masc. singular noun** (before noun, drops -o)
- `Algún día.` — Some day.
- `¿Tienes algún problema?` — Do you have some/any problem?

**alguno** — standing alone (masculine, after noun or without one)
- `¿Tienes alguno?` — Do you have any/some?

**alguna + fem. singular noun**
- `Alguna vez.` — Some time / at some point.

**algunos/algunas + plural noun**
- `Algunos estudiantes.` — Some students.
- `Algunas veces.` — Sometimes.

| Form | Use |
|---|---|
| `algo` | something (pronoun, no noun) |
| `algún` | some + masc. singular noun |
| `alguna` | some + fem. singular noun |
| `algunos/as` | some + plural noun |
