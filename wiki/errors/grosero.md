## What Goes Wrong

The word `grosero` is repeatedly forgotten. It is mistaken for or replaced with `maleducado`, or the word simply doesn't come to mind when needed.

## Why It Happens

Both `grosero` and `maleducado` mean rude, but they have a nuance difference that makes `grosero` less "sticky":
- `maleducado` = badly brought up, ill-mannered (default go-to word for rude)
- `grosero` = crude, crass, deliberately vulgar or offensive — a stronger, more active rudeness

Because `maleducado` covers most everyday uses of "rude," `grosero` doesn't get reinforced.

## Correct Usage

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `maleducado` | ill-mannered, rude (general) | *No seas maleducado.* — Don't be rude. |
| `grosero` | crude, crass, vulgar | *Fue muy grosero con el camarero.* — He was very rude (crass) to the waiter. |

Memory hook: **GROSEro** sounds like "gross" — it's the grosser, more offensive kind of rude.
