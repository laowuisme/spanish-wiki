# masculine-feminine agreement

## What Goes Wrong

Adjectives, articles, demonstratives, and possessives must agree in gender and number with the noun they modify. The errors documented in your notes:

- Using `esta` (feminine) with a masculine noun (`este musical`, not `esta musical`)
- `mucho gente` instead of `mucha gente` (gente is feminine)
- `calcetín` is masculine — saying `otros calcetines` (correct) but initially used wrong article/agreement

## Why It Happens

Spanish gender is not always predictable from the noun ending. Common traps:
- Words ending in `-a` assumed to be feminine but masculine (traitor nouns: `el problema`, `el idioma`, `el día`)
- Nouns of foreign origin where gender is not intuitive
- `gente` (people) looks like it might be masculine or neutral — it is feminine singular

The error cascades: if you misremember the gender of a noun, every modifier attached to it will also be wrong.

## Correct Usage

| Mistake | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `esta musical` | `este musical` | musical is masculine |
| `mucho gente` | `mucha gente` | gente is feminine |
| `otros calcetines` — already correct (calcetín is masculine) | `No hay otros calcetines.` | confirmed correct |
| `la problema` | `el problema` | Greek -ma nouns are masculine |

Rule of thumb:
- Default article for unknown nouns: guess masculine (`el`)
- Check traitor nouns list — see [[traitor-nouns]]
