# cuál vs qué — Which vs What

## Rule

Both `cuál` (pl. `cuáles`) and `qué` translate loosely as "what" or "which" in English, but they are not interchangeable in Spanish.

**qué** is used when asking for a definition, explanation, or classification. It asks "what kind of thing is this?"
- `¿Qué es esto?` — What is this?
- `¿Qué haces?` — What are you doing?

**cuál / cuáles** is used when selecting from a set of options, or asking for a specific item from within a known category. It implies "which one(s)?":
- `¿Cuál es tu nombre?` — What is your name? (selecting from names)
- `¿Cuál de estas?` — Which of these?
- `¿Cuáles de estos?` — Which of these (plural)?

A key practical rule: before `es/son`, Spanish generally prefers `cuál` when pointing to a specific identity, not `qué`.

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `¿Qué + verb?` | Ask about action or definition | `¿Qué quieres?` |
| `¿Qué es + noun?` | Define something | `¿Qué es un sofá?` |
| `¿Cuál es + noun?` | Select a specific item | `¿Cuál es tu apellido?` |
| `¿Cuál de estas?` | Choose among options | `¿Cuál de estas te gusta?` |
| `¿Cuáles de estos?` | Plural selection | `¿Cuáles de estos son tuyos?` |
