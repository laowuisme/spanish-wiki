## Rule

Spanish has a spectrum for expressing affection and love, unlike English where "I love you" covers everything. From lightest to strongest:

| Expression | Literal | Meaning in context |
|---|---|---|
| *Me gustas* | You please me | I'm attracted to you / I like you (romantically) |
| *Me encantas* | You enchant me | I really like you / I'm crazy about you |
| *Te quiero* | I want you | I love you (most common; used for partners, family, close friends) |
| *Te amo* | I love you | I love you (deeper, more solemn; reserved for serious romance or family) |

## Key Distinctions

**Te quiero vs Te amo:**
- *Te quiero* is the everyday "I love you" in most of Latin America and Spain. You can say it to a partner, a parent, a best friend.
- *Te amo* is weightier and more formal — declaring a deep, lasting love. In some countries (Mexico, for example), it's also used with family. In others, it's strictly romantic.
- When in doubt with a partner: *te quiero* is safe and warm; *te amo* is a bigger declaration.

**Me gustas vs Me encantas:**
- *Me gustas* = I'm attracted to you / I like you (attraction, early stages). Not "I love you."
- *Me encantas* = I really like/love you (stronger than *me gustas*, but less committed than *te quiero*).
- Note: *Me gusta* (no *-s*) = I like it/something, not a person. The *-s* marks a person as the subject.

## Common Patterns

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Telling a friend "love you!" | *¡Te quiero!* |
| Telling a partner "I love you" (daily) | *Te quiero* or *Te amo* depending on country/moment |
| First time confessing feelings | *Me gustas mucho* → *Te quiero* |
| Saying "I love your cooking" (thing, not person) | *Me encanta tu cocina* |
| "I love dogs" (category) | *Me encantan los perros* |
