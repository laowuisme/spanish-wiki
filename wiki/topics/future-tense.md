# Future Tense — ir a + infinitive vs Simple Future

## Rule

Spanish has two main ways to express the future:

### 1. Periphrastic Future: ir a + infinitive
This is the most common spoken future, especially for near or planned events. It mirrors English "going to":

`ir (conjugated) + a + infinitive`

| Person | ir | Example |
|---|---|---|
| yo | voy | `Voy a comer.` — I'm going to eat. |
| tú | vas | `Vas a estudiar.` — You're going to study. |
| él/ella | va | `Va a llover.` — It's going to rain. |
| nosotros | vamos | `Vamos a salir.` — We're going to go out. |
| vosotros | vais | `Vais a llegar tarde.` — You're going to arrive late. |
| ellos | van | `Van a comprar flores.` — They're going to buy flowers. |

### 2. Simple Future (Futuro Simple)
Formed by adding endings to the infinitive. Used for more distant, hypothetical, or formal future statements, and also for conjecture about the present ("must be"):

Endings (same for all verbs): `-é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án`

| Person | hablar | comer | vivir |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | hablaré | comeré | viviré |
| tú | hablarás | comerás | vivirás |
| él/ella | hablará | comerá | vivirá |
| nosotros | hablaremos | comeremos | viviremos |
| vosotros | hablaréis | comeréis | viviréis |
| ellos | hablarán | comerán | vivirán |

Note: Some common verbs have irregular stems in the simple future: `tener→tendr-`, `poder→podr-`, `venir→vendr-`, `salir→saldr-`, `hacer→har-`, `querer→querr-`.

### Contrast

- `Voy a comprar flores.` — I'm going to buy flowers. (near/planned)
- `Compraré flores.` — I will buy flowers. (more formal/hypothetical)

`la semana que viene` (the coming week) is another common near-future construction.

## Common Patterns

- `estoy a punto de + infinitive` — I'm about to do something (immediate future)
- `la semana que viene` — next week
