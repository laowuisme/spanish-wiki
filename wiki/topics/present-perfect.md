# Present Perfect — He hablado

## Rule

The present perfect (*pretérito perfecto compuesto*) names a past action that still connects to now. It has two parts: the present tense of `haber` and a past participle.

### Part 1: `haber` in the present

| Person | haber |
|---|---|
| yo | he |
| tú | has |
| él / ella / usted | ha |
| nosotros | hemos |
| vosotros | habéis |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | han |

The note of 2026-10-29 lists `he/has/ha/hemo`. The list stops early. You confirmed that `hemo` means `hemos` and that `han` is missing.

### Part 2: the past participle

| Verb ending | Participle ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -ar | -ado | hablar → hablado, escalar → escalado |
| -er | -ido | comer → comido, pedir → pedido |
| -ir | -ido | vivir → vivido, conseguir → conseguido |

These participles are irregular. You must learn them one by one:

| Verb | Participle |
|---|---|
| hacer | hecho |
| decir | dicho |
| ver | visto |
| escribir | escrito |
| poner | puesto |
| volver | vuelto |
| abrir | abierto |
| romper | roto |

### Three rules that prevent mistakes

1. In this tense the participle always ends in `-o`. It does not agree with the subject: `Ellas han salido`, not ~~han salidas~~.
2. Nothing goes between `haber` and the participle. Put `no` and the pronouns before `haber`: `No lo he visto.` `Me he levantado temprano.`
3. Use `haber`, not `tener`: `He comido`, not ~~tengo comido~~.

### The participle as an adjective

A participle can also work as an adjective. It then agrees with the noun, because it is no longer part of the tense. `neumático pinchado` (flat tire) is an example: `pinchado` is the participle of `pinchar`. Compare `un neumático pinchado`, `una rueda pinchada`, `dos neumáticos pinchados` with `Han pinchado dos neumáticos`, where the participle stays `pinchado`.

### When to use it

- **Experience up to now.** The question word is often `alguna vez`, and the answer often uses `nunca`, `ya` or `todavía no`.
- **A period that is not finished.** Typical time words are `hoy`, `esta semana`, `este año`.
- **Recent news that matters now.**

Regional use differs. In Spain, speakers use the present perfect for anything earlier today. In much of Latin America, speakers often use the preterite for the same idea (`Hoy trabajé mucho`). Both forms are correct. This point is not from your notes.

### What it does not do

English says "I have lived here for two years". Spanish uses the present tense with `desde hace`: `Vivo aquí desde hace dos años`. See [[desde-hace]].

For a finished period (`ayer`, `el año pasado`) use the preterite. See [[past-tense]].

## Common Patterns

These examples were written for this page. They are not from your notes.

| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| experience + `alguna vez` | `¿Has estado alguna vez en el Caribe?` | Have you ever been to the Caribbean? |
| `nunca` + `he` | `Nunca he escalado una montaña.` | I have never climbed a mountain. |
| `nosotros` (`hemos`) | `Hemos pedido la cuenta.` | We have asked for the bill. |
| `ella` (`ha`) | `Ella ha conseguido un buen trabajo.` | She has got a good job. |
| `ellos` (`han`) | `Mis amigos mochileros han viajado por todo el Caribe.` | My backpacker friends have travelled all around the Caribbean. |
| irregular participle | `¿Has hecho la tarea?` | Have you done the homework? |
| `todavía no` | `Todavía no he comido.` | I have not eaten yet. |
| pronoun before `haber` | `No lo he visto.` | I have not seen it. |
| two pronouns before `haber` | `Se lo he dado.` | I have given it to him/her. |
