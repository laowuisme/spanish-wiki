# antigua vs vieja — Two words for "old"

## Rule

Both *antiguo/a* and *viejo/a* translate as "old" in English but are not interchangeable.

**antiguo/a** — old in the sense of ancient, antique, former, long-established
- Used for things with historical age, past roles, or long tradition.
- *la ciudad antigua* — the old (ancient) city
- *mi antigua escuela* — my former school
- *un mueble antiguo* — an antique piece of furniture

**viejo/a** — old in the sense of worn, aged, or familiar
- Used for things that are worn out, or people who are elderly.
- Can sound affectionate or slightly pejorative depending on context.
- *un coche viejo* — an old (worn) car
- *mi viejo amigo* — my old friend (long-standing)
- *un hombre viejo* — an old man (neutral/descriptive)

## The Test

Ask: is it *historically old / formerly something*? → *antiguo*
Is it *worn / simply aged*? → *viejo*

## Common Patterns

| Context | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Historical age | *antiguo* | *Roma es una ciudad antigua.* |
| Former role | *antiguo* | *mi antiguo jefe* — my former boss |
| Worn / aged object | *viejo* | *un libro viejo* |
| Elderly person | *viejo* | *un señor viejo* |
| Old friend (fondly) | *viejo* | *¡Hola, viejo!* (colloquial) |
