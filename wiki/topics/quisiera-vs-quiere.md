# quisiera vs quiere — Polite Request vs Simple Want

## Rule

`quiere` is the third-person singular present indicative of `querer`. It states a direct desire or want as a fact.
- `Ella quiere agua.` — She wants water.

`quisiera` is the first/third-person singular imperfect subjunctive of `querer`. In everyday usage it functions as a polite conditional ("I would like"), softening requests.
- `Quisiera un café, por favor.` — I would like a coffee, please.

The imperfect subjunctive form `quisiera` is used exactly like English "would like" — it is softer and more polite than the blunt present tense. In formal or service contexts (restaurants, shops), always prefer `quisiera`.

## Common Patterns

| Form | Person | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `quiero` | yo | `Quiero agua.` — I want water. |
| `quieres` | tú | `¿Quieres salir?` — Do you want to go out? |
| `quiere` | él/ella/usted | `Ella quiere ir.` — She wants to go. |
| `quisiera` | yo / él / usted | `Quisiera reservar una mesa.` — I'd like to book a table. |

The full present tense conjugation of `querer` (irregular e→ie stem change):
`quiero, quieres, quiere, queremos, queréis, quieren`
