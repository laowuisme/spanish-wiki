## Rule

*Nadie* (nobody) and *nada* (nothing) follow the same double-negation rule as *ningún*: when placed **after** the verb, *no* must still appear before the verb.

> *No trabaja nadie por la mañana.* — Nobody works in the morning.
> *No hay nada aquí.* — There's nothing here.

When *nadie* or *nada* appear **before** the verb, *no* is dropped:

> *Nadie trabaja por la mañana.* — Nobody works in the morning. ✓
> *Nada me importa.* — Nothing matters to me. ✓

Both forms are correct — the word order determines whether *no* is needed.

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| no + verb + nadie | *No llama nadie.* | Nobody calls. |
| nadie + verb | *Nadie llama.* | Same meaning, no double negation needed. |
| no + verb + nada | *No como nada.* | I don't eat anything / I eat nothing. |
| nada + verb | *Nada me gusta.* | Nothing pleases me. |
