## Rule

*Menos* (less/fewer) is used to form "less than" comparisons and to express reduced quantity with nouns:

> *Hay menos gente allí.* — There are fewer people there.
> *Tengo menos dinero que tú.* — I have less money than you.

*Más* (more) and *menos* (less/fewer) are the two quantity comparators. Both work with nouns, adjectives, and adverbs.

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| menos + noun | *Hay menos gente.* | There are fewer people. |
| menos + adj/adv | *Es menos difícil.* | It's less difficult. |
| menos... que | *Habla menos que yo.* | She speaks less than I do. |
| cada vez menos | *Hay cada vez menos trabajo.* | There's less and less work. |

**Exceptions — irregular comparatives:**
- *bueno* → *mejor* (better), not *más bueno*
- *malo* → *peor* (worse), not *más malo*
- *poco* → *menos* (less) — *menos* IS the comparative of *poco*

**Note:** *menos* doesn't change for gender or number.
