## Rule

*Hay que + infinitive* expresses impersonal obligation — something that must be done, without specifying who must do it. It is the impersonal equivalent of *tener que*.

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *hay que + infinitive* | one must / you have to (general) | *Hay que comer bien.* — One must eat well. |
| *tener que + infinitive* | I/you/he have to (specific person) | *Tengo que comer bien.* — I have to eat well. |

*Hay que* never changes form — it is always third-person singular and has no subject. It is used for general rules, advice, and instructions.

## Common Patterns

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| *Hay que descansar.* | One must rest. / You need to rest. |
| *Hay que llamar antes.* | You have to call ahead. |
| *Hay que evitar el alcohol.* | Alcohol must be avoided. |
| *No hay que preocuparse.* | There's no need to worry. |
