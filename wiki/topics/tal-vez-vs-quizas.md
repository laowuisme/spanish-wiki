# tal vez vs quizás — Both mean "maybe"

## Rule

*Tal vez* and *quizás* (also *quizá*) are synonyms — both mean "maybe / perhaps." Neither is wrong in any context; the choice is largely stylistic.

**Shared behaviour:** When placed **before** the verb, both can trigger the subjunctive (expressing doubt) or take the indicative (stating a possibility). The subjunctive is more formal.

> *Quizás venga mañana.* — Maybe he'll come tomorrow. (subjunctive — more uncertain)
> *Quizás viene mañana.* — Maybe he's coming tomorrow. (indicative — slightly more certain)
> *Tal vez llueva esta tarde.* — Maybe it'll rain this afternoon. (subjunctive)
> *Tal vez llueve esta tarde.* — Maybe it'll rain this afternoon. (indicative)

When placed **after** the verb, both take the indicative only.

## Common Patterns

| Expression | Notes |
|---|---|
| *quizás / quizá* | Both spellings correct; interchangeable |
| *tal vez* | Always two words; equivalent to quizás |
| *a lo mejor* | Colloquial synonym — always takes indicative, never subjunctive |

**Memory hook:** *A lo mejor* = always indicative. *Quizás / tal vez* = indicative or subjunctive depending on mood.
