# Indirect Object — A + name/pronoun + le/te/me

## Rule

In Spanish, when the indirect object (the person who receives or is affected by the action) is a specific person or pronoun, it is introduced by the preposition **a** and must also be doubled with an indirect object pronoun (le, te, me, etc.).

This doubling is mandatory — you cannot drop the pronoun even when the full noun is present.

### Structure
`A + [person/pronoun] + [IOP] + verb`

| Sentence | Breakdown |
|---|---|
| `A Felipe le encanta dibujar.` | A Felipe = clarifies who; le = required IOP |
| `A ti te encanta el fútbol.` | A ti = clarifies who (tú); te = required IOP |
| `A mí me gusta el café.` | A mí = clarifies who (yo); me = required IOP |

### Why `a ti te` and not just `le gusta`?
- `Le gusta el fútbol` would be ambiguous — `le` could mean he, she, or you (usted).
- `A ti te encanta el fútbol` is explicit: specifically **you** (tú) are referenced.
- With `tú`, you use `te`, not `le`. The `a ti` is added for emphasis/clarity.

### Indirect Object Pronoun summary

| Person | IOP | Stress form (with a) |
|---|---|---|
| yo | me | a mí me |
| tú | te | a ti te |
| él/ella/usted | le | a él/ella/usted le |
| nosotros | nos | a nosotros nos |
| vosotros | os | a vosotros os |
| ellos/ustedes | les | a ellos/ustedes les |

## Common Patterns

- `A + name + le gusta/encanta + noun/infinitive`
- `A ti te gusta / a mí me gusta` (for contrast or emphasis)

## Your Mistakes

- Confusion: "Why can't we say `le gusta el fútbol` when talking about `ti`?" — Because `le` doesn't correspond to tú. Use `te` for tú.
