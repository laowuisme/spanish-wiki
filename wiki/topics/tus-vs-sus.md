# tus vs sus — Possessive Adjectives (your/their/his/her)

## Rule

Both `tus` and `sus` are plural possessive adjectives (used with plural nouns), but they belong to different persons:

| Form | Singular | Plural | Refers to |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tu` | tu libro | tus libros | your (informal, tú) |
| `su` | su libro | sus libros | his / her / its / your (formal, usted) / their |

`tus` = **your** (informal, plural noun). It belongs to the second person singular (tú).
`sus` = **his/her/its/their/your (formal)** (plural noun). It belongs to the third person or formal second person.

The ambiguity of `sus` (it can mean his, her, their, or formal your) is resolved by context. If clarity is needed, Spanish adds a clarifying phrase: `sus libros de él` (his books).

## Common Patterns

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| `tus zapatos` | your shoes |
| `sus zapatos` | his/her/their shoes |
| `tus amigos` | your friends |
| `sus amigos` | his/her/their/your (formal) friends |

Note: possessive adjectives agree with the noun they modify (the thing owned), not with the owner's gender.
