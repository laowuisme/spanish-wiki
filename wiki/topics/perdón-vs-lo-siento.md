# perdón vs lo siento — Excuse Me vs I'm Sorry

## Rule

Both `perdón` and `lo siento` express apology, but they differ in gravity and function:

**perdón** — "Excuse me" / mild apology or request to pass
- Used when bumping into someone, getting attention, asking someone to repeat themselves.
- Also used as a light "sorry" for minor inconveniences.
- `Perdón, ¿puede repetir?` — Excuse me, could you repeat that?
- `¡Perdón!` — Excuse me! (when squeezing past someone)

**lo siento** — "I'm sorry" / genuine empathy or regret
- Used for actual sympathy, regret, or a meaningful apology.
- `Lo siento mucho.` — I'm very sorry.
- `Lo siento, no puedo ir.` — I'm sorry, I can't go.

A useful analogy: `perdón` ≈ "excuse me" or "pardon", `lo siento` ≈ "I'm sorry."

## Common Patterns

| Situation | Use | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| Bumping into someone | perdón | `¡Perdón!` |
| Getting someone's attention | perdón | `Perdón, ¿dónde está...?` |
| Expressing sympathy | lo siento | `Lo siento mucho.` |
| Apologising for a real mistake | lo siento | `Lo siento, me equivoqué.` |

## perdón vs perdona

A second distinction worth noting within *perdón*:

**perdón** (noun) — used as an exclamation, more formal
- *¡Perdón!* — Excuse me! / Pardon!

**perdona** (imperative of *perdonar*) — "forgive me", more informal/direct
- *Perdona, ¿me puedes ayudar?* — Sorry, can you help me? (tú, informal)
- *Perdone* (usted form) — Excuse me (formal, to a stranger)

In practice: *perdona* feels warmer and more personal; *perdón* is shorter and more like a quick "sorry" or "excuse me."
