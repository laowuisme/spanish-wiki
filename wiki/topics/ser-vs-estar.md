# Ser vs Estar

## Rule

Spanish has two verbs for "to be," and the choice depends on whether you're describing something permanent/inherent or something temporary/circumstantial.

- **ser** → identity, characteristics, origin, profession, material, time, inherent traits ("what something *is*")
- **estar** → location, temporary states/conditions, ongoing actions (with gerund) ("how something *is right now*")

A useful test: if the quality is something you'd list to describe *who someone is*, use **ser**. If it's a state that could change moment to moment, use **estar**.

## Common Patterns

| Use | Ser | Estar |
|---|---|---|
| Characteristic/trait | *Es honesto.* — He's honest (as a person). | — |
| Temporary mood/state | — | *Está feliz.* — He's happy (right now). |
| Identity/profession | *Es profesor.* — He's a teacher. | — |
| Location | — | *Está en casa.* — He's at home. |
| Origin/material | *Es de España.* / *Es de madera.* | — |
| Ongoing action | — | *Está comiendo.* — He's eating. |
