# Time Representation — Telling the Time in Spanish

## Rule

Telling the time uses `ser` (to be) with the definite article `la` (for 1 o'clock) or `las` (for all other hours), because the word `hora(s)` is feminine.

### Basic structure
- `Es la una.` — It's one o'clock. (singular: only one o'clock uses `la`)
- `Son las dos.` — It's two o'clock.
- `Son las seis.` — It's six o'clock.

### With a specific time (scheduled events)
Add `a` to express "at a time":
- `Es a las seis de la mañana.` — It's at six in the morning.
- `A la una en punto.` — At one o'clock sharp.

### Exclamatory / surprise
- `¡Las cuatro de la mañana ya!` — It's already four in the morning! (No `a` — this is an exclamation, not a scheduled event)

### Time of day modifiers
- `de la mañana` — in the morning (a.m.)
- `de la tarde` — in the afternoon/evening
- `de la noche` — at night

### Minutes
- `Son las tres y media.` — It's half past three. (y media = and a half)
- `Son las tres y cuarto.` — It's quarter past three. (y cuarto = and a quarter)
- `Son las tres menos cuarto.` — It's quarter to three. (menos = minus)

## Common Patterns

| Expression | Translation |
|---|---|
| `Es la una.` | It's one o'clock. |
| `Son las seis.` | It's six o'clock. |
| `A la una en punto` | At one o'clock sharp |
| `¡Las cuatro ya!` | It's already four! |
| `de la mañana/tarde/noche` | a.m. / p.m. / at night |
