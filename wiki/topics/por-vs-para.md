# por vs para — Purpose, Cause, and Exchange

## Rule

Both `por` and `para` can translate as "for" in English, but they have distinct uses.

### por — reason, cause, exchange, duration, frequency, on behalf of

| Use | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| Reason/cause | `por eso no tomo clases` | that's why I don't take classes |
| Frequency | `dos veces por mes` | twice a month |
| Duration | `estudié por dos horas` | I studied for two hours |
| Exchange | `lo compré por diez euros` | I bought it for ten euros |
| On behalf of | `lo hago por ti` | I do it for you (for your sake) |

### para — purpose/goal, destination, deadline, recipient

| Use | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | `estudio para aprender` | I study in order to learn |
| Destination | `voy para Madrid` | I'm heading to Madrid |
| Deadline | `lo necesito para el lunes` | I need it by Monday |
| Recipient | `esto es para ti` | this is for you (as a gift/recipient) |

### porque vs por qué vs porque
- `porque` — because (conjunction, answers "why?")
- `por qué` — why? (question word, two words with accent)
- `por que` — less common, used in specific grammatical structures

## Common Patterns

- `por eso` — therefore / that's why
- `dos veces por mes / semana` — twice a month / week
- `por supuesto` — of course (fixed expression)

## Your Mistakes

- `mucho gente lo hace` — correct is `mucha gente` (gente is feminine)
- `porque vs por que` — flagged as confusion point
