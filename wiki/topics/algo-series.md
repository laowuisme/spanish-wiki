## Rule

Spanish has a family of indefinite pronouns that form pairs: affirmative (something/someone/any) vs negative (nothing/nobody/none). Mastering these together is the most efficient path.

## The Full Table

| Meaning | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| something / nothing | *algo* | *nada* |
| someone / nobody | *alguien* | *nadie* |
| some + noun (masc.) | *algún* / *alguno* | *ningún* / *ninguno* |
| some + noun (fem.) | *alguna* | *ninguna* |
| some (plural) | *algunos / algunas* | — (ningún/ninguna are singular only) |

## Key Rules

**1. Double negation:** Negative forms after the verb require *no* before the verb.
> *No hay nada.* / *Nada hay.* (both correct — see [[nadie-nada-negation]], [[no-tiene-hay-ningun]])

**2. Algún/ningún lose the -o before masculine nouns:**
> *algún libro* (some book) — NOT *alguno libro*
> *ningún problema* (no problem) — NOT *ninguno problema*
> But: *alguno de ellos* (one of them) — kept when not before a noun

**3. Alguien/nadie refer only to people and take personal a as direct objects:**
> *¿Ves a alguien?* — Do you see anyone?
> *No veo a nadie.* — I don't see anyone. (See [[personal-a]])

**4. Ningún/ninguna are almost always singular:**
> *No tengo ningún amigo aquí.* (not *ningunos amigos*)

## Common Patterns

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| *¿Hay algo de comer?* | Is there anything to eat? |
| *No hay nada de comer.* | There's nothing to eat. |
| *¿Conoces a alguien aquí?* | Do you know anyone here? |
| *No conozco a nadie.* | I don't know anyone. |
| *¿Tienes algún problema?* | Do you have any problem? |
| *No tengo ningún problema.* | I have no problem. |
| *Algunos estudiantes llegaron.* | Some students arrived. |
