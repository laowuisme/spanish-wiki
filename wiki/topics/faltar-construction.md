## Rule

*Faltar* ("to lack, to be missing") follows the same reverse-subject construction as *gustar*: the person who experiences the lack takes an indirect object pronoun, and the thing or person that is missing is the grammatical subject the verb agrees with.

> *Faltan Diego y Ana.* — Diego and Ana are missing. (literally: "Diego and Ana are lacking")

The verb form matches the subject that follows it (*falta* for singular, *faltan* for plural) — not the person experiencing the lack.

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| (A alguien) le falta + singular noun | *Me falta tiempo.* | I lack time. / I'm short on time. |
| (A alguien) le faltan + plural noun | *Faltan Diego y Ana.* | Diego and Ana are missing. |
| hace falta + infinitive/noun | *Hace falta paciencia.* | Patience is needed. |
