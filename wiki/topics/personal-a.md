# Personal a — Required Before Direct Object Persons

## Rule

In Spanish, when the direct object of a verb is a specific person (or a personified animal/entity), you must place **a** immediately before it. This is called the "personal a" — it has no equivalent in English and is often invisible to English speakers.

- `Conozco a tu esposo.` — I know your husband. (esposo is a specific person → personal a required)
- `Visito a mis padres.` — I visit my parents. (padres are specific people → personal a required)
- `Busco un apartamento.` — I'm looking for an apartment. (thing, not person → no personal a)
- `Busco a mi hermano.` — I'm looking for my brother. (specific person → personal a required)

### When to use personal a:
1. After verbs like `conocer`, `visitar`, `ver`, `llamar`, `escuchar`, `ayudar`, `querer` when the object is a specific person.
2. With pets: `Quiero a mi perro.`
3. With pronouns referring to people: `alguien`, `nadie`, `quién`, personal pronouns.

### When NOT to use personal a:
- Objects that are things, places, or abstract concepts.
- After `tener`: `Tengo un hermano.` (NOT `Tengo a un hermano` — general reference)
- After `haber`: `Hay médicos aquí.` (existential)

## Common Patterns

| Verb | Example with personal a | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| conocer | `conoce a tu esposo` | he knows your husband |
| visitar | `visito a mis padres` | I visit my parents |
| llamar | `llamé a María` | I called María |
| ver | `vi a Pedro` | I saw Pedro |
| buscar | `busco a mi amigo` | I'm looking for my friend |
