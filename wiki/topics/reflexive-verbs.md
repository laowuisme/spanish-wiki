# Reflexive Verbs — me lavo, te cepillas, se ducha...

## Rule

Reflexive verbs in Spanish describe actions that the subject does to themselves. They are marked by reflexive pronouns (`me, te, se, nos, os, se`) placed before the conjugated verb.

The infinitive form always ends in `-se`: `lavarse`, `ducharse`, `cepillarse`, `bañarse`, `quedarse`, `peinarse`.

### Reflexive pronoun chart

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| yo | me | `Me lavo la cara.` — I wash my face. |
| tú | te | `Te cepillas los dientes.` — You brush your teeth. |
| él/ella | se | `Se ducha.` — He/she showers. |
| nosotros | nos | `Nos bañamos.` — We bathe. |
| vosotros | os | `Os laváis.` — You all wash. |
| ellos | se | `Se peinan.` — They comb their hair. |

### Body part with reflexive verbs
When a reflexive verb involves a body part, Spanish uses the definite article (`el/la/los/las`) — NOT the possessive adjective:
- `Me lavo **la** cara.` (NOT `mi cara`)
- `Te cepillas **los** dientes.` (NOT `tus dientes`)

This is because the reflexive pronoun already indicates possession.

### Placement rules
- Before conjugated verb: `Me ducho.`
- Attached to infinitive: `Quiero ducharme.` or `Me quiero duchar.`
- Attached to gerund: `Estoy duchándome.` or `Me estoy duchando.`

### `ducharse` vs `se duchar`
The correct infinitive form is `ducharse` (ending attached). `se duchar` is incorrect. However, `se` can detach and precede auxiliary verbs: `me quiero duchar` = `quiero ducharme`.

## Common Patterns

| Verb | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| lavarse | `Me lavo las manos.` | I wash my hands. |
| ducharse | `Se ducha cada día.` | He/she showers every day. |
| cepillarse | `Te cepillas los dientes.` | You brush your teeth. |
| bañarse | `Nosotros nos bañamos por la noche.` | We bathe at night. |
| peinarse | `Se peinan.` | They comb their hair. |
| quedarse | `¿Te quedas?` | Are you staying? |
| confundirse | `Siempre me confundo.` | I always get confused. |

### Intensive (aspectual) se

`se` can also appear with transitive verbs (verbs that take a direct object) to express **completeness or total involvement** in an action — the subject fully consumes or finishes something. This is not reflexive in the usual sense.

| Example | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `Lin se come un pedazo de pizza.` | Lin eats (up) a piece of pizza. | Emphasises she eats the whole piece |
| `Me bebí el café.` | I drank (all of) the coffee. | Completeness of consumption |
| `Se leyó el libro en un día.` | He read the whole book in one day. | Total completion |

The intensive `se` is optional — `Lin come un pedazo de pizza` is also correct — but adding `se` adds a sense of intentional, complete action.
