# Traitor Nouns — Misleading Gender Endings

## Rule

In Spanish, nouns ending in `-a` are usually feminine and nouns ending in `-o` are usually masculine. "Traitor nouns" are exceptions that break this pattern. They must be memorised individually.

### Masculine nouns ending in -a (appear feminine, are masculine)

These are mostly borrowed from Greek and end in `-ma`, `-pa`, or `-ta`:

| Noun | Translation | Article |
|---|---|---|
| el día | the day | masculine |
| el mapa | the map | masculine |
| el problema | the problem | masculine |
| el idioma | the language/idiom | masculine |
| el sofá | the sofa | masculine |
| el poema | the poem | masculine |
| el programa | the program | masculine |
| el tema | the theme/topic | masculine |
| el sistema | the system | masculine |

### Feminine nouns ending in -o (appear masculine, are feminine)

This is a very short list — essentially one dominant example:

| Noun | Translation | Article |
|---|---|---|
| la mano | the hand | feminine |

(Also: `la foto` — short for `fotografía`, `la moto` — short for `motocicleta`)

## Common Patterns

- Words of Greek origin ending in `-ma` are almost always masculine.
- `la mano` is the classic feminine `-o` exception — it comes from Latin `manus` (feminine).
- Adjectives and articles must agree with the actual gender, not the apparent gender of the ending.
