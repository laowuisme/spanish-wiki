## Rule

Both *lamentablemente* and *desafortunadamente* mean "unfortunately" and are largely interchangeable. The distinction is subtle and contextual:

**lamentablemente** — "regrettably"; implies something worthy of grief or mourning
- From *lamentar* (to lament/regret). Slightly more formal or literary.
- *Lamentablemente, no pude asistir.* — Regrettably, I couldn't attend.

**desafortunadamente** — "unfortunately"; implies bad luck or an unfavourable outcome
- From *afortunado* (fortunate) + *des-* (un-). More neutral in register.
- *Desafortunadamente, el vuelo se canceló.* — Unfortunately, the flight was cancelled.

In everyday speech, both are used interchangeably. Neither is wrong in most contexts.

## Common Patterns

| Word | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *lamentablemente* | more formal/emotional | *Lamentablemente, no hay solución.* |
| *desafortunadamente* | neutral | *Desafortunadamente, llegué tarde.* |
| *por desgracia* | colloquial equivalent | *Por desgracia, no funciona.* |
