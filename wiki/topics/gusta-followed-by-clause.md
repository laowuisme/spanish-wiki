# gustar followed by a clause (cuando, que + clause)

## Rule

`gustar` (and similar verbs: `encantar`, `interesar`, `molestar`, etc.) take the thing liked as their grammatical subject. When what you like is an action or a clause rather than a noun, the clause itself acts as the subject.

**gustar + infinitive** (when you like doing something):
- `Me gusta bailar.` — I like dancing. (the infinitive `bailar` is the subject)

**gustar + que + subjunctive** (when you like that someone else does something):
- `Me gusta que vengas.` — I like that you come.

**gustar + cuando + indicative** (when you like it when something happens):
- `No me gusta cuando hay gente.` — I don't like it when there are people.

In the `cuando` construction, note that `hay` (there is/are) stays in indicative. The verb `gustar` stays singular because the entire clause (`cuando hay gente`) acts as one subject unit.

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| me gusta + infinitive | `Me gusta correr.` | I like running. |
| me gusta cuando... | `No me gusta cuando llueve.` | I don't like when it rains. |
| me encanta + infinitive | `Me encanta ir al campo.` | I love going to the countryside. |
| le interesa + infinitive | `Le interesa aprender.` | He/she is interested in learning. |
| le interesan + plural noun | `Le interesan los videojuegos.` | He's interested in video games. |

The verb agrees with the subject (the thing liked), not the person:
- `Me gusta el café.` (singular noun → gusta)
- `Me gustan los cafés.` (plural noun → gustan)
- `Me gusta cantar.` (infinitive → always gusta)
