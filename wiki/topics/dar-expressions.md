## Rule

**Dar + noun** is used to express feelings or reactions that something *gives* you. The subject is the thing causing the feeling; the indirect object pronoun (me/te/le/nos/les) marks who feels it.

Structure: **[pronoun] + da/dan + [noun]** → *Me da vergüenza* = It makes me ashamed / I'm embarrassed by it.

Compare with [[tener-expressions]] where *tener* takes the noun directly (*tengo miedo* = I'm afraid), while *dar* needs the indirect object pronoun.

## Common Patterns

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Me da vergüenza | I'm embarrassed (by it) |
| Me da miedo | It scares me / I'm afraid of it |
| Me da igual | I don't mind / it's all the same to me |
| Me da asco | It disgusts me / I find it gross |
| Me da pena | It makes me sad / I feel sorry |
| Me da risa | It makes me laugh |

**Plural subject** → *dan*: *Me dan miedo los truenos.* = Thunder scares me.
