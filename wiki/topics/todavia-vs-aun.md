# todavía vs aún

## Rule

Both *todavía* and *aún* mean **still** (in affirmative) or **yet** (in negative). They are largely interchangeable in most contexts. The key difference is register and the accent:

- *aún* (with accent) = still/yet — interchangeable with *todavía*
- *aun* (no accent) = even — a completely different word

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| todavía + verb | *Todavía estoy aquí.* | I'm still here. |
| aún + verb | *Aún estoy aquí.* | I'm still here. |
| todavía no | *Todavía no he comido.* | I haven't eaten yet. |
| aún no | *Aún no he comido.* | I haven't eaten yet. |
| aun (no accent) + adj | *Aun así, es difícil.* | Even so, it's difficult. |

**Affirmative (still):** both *todavía* and *aún* work equally.

**Negative (yet):** *todavía no* and *aún no* both mean "not yet."

**Accent trap:** *aun* without accent = "even" (e.g., *aun sin dinero* = even without money). Always accent *aún* when you mean "still/yet."
