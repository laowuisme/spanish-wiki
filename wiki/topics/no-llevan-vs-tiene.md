# llevar vs tener — To Carry/Wear/Contain vs To Have

## Rule

`llevar` and `tener` both can translate as "to have" in certain English contexts, but they have distinct meanings:

**tener** — to have (possession, inherent quality, personal ownership)
- `Ella tiene otro vestido.` — She has another dress.
- `Tengo un problema.` — I have a problem.

**llevar** — to carry, to wear, to take (somewhere), to contain (as ingredients)
- `Llevo una mochila.` — I'm carrying/wearing a backpack.
- `No llevan chiles.` — They don't have chiles (i.e., the dish doesn't contain / they don't use chiles as an ingredient).
- `Me llevo bien con ella.` — I get along well with her.

### Why "No llevan chiles" and not "No tienen chiles"?
When talking about ingredients in a dish, or what a place/recipe includes, Spanish uses `llevar` — the sense is "to carry/include as a component", not mere possession. `Tener` would imply the place owns chiles; `llevar` implies the dish contains them.

### Other llevar patterns
- `llevar + time` = to have been doing something for a period: `Llevo dos años estudiando español.` — I've been studying Spanish for two years.
- `llevar + worn item` = to be wearing: `Lleva un sombrero.` — He's wearing a hat.

## Common Patterns

| Context | llevar | tener |
|---|---|---|
| Wearing | `Lleva gafas.` (wearing glasses) | — |
| Ingredients | `No lleva sal.` (no salt in it) | — |
| Duration | `Llevo años aquí.` (been here for years) | — |
| Possession | — | `Tiene un coche.` |
| State | — | `Tiene frío.` |

## Your Mistakes

- Confusion between `llega` and `llevar` — see [[confusing-llevar-llegar]]
- Using `tiene` when `lleva` is needed for ingredients/contents.
