# al — Contraction of a + el

## Rule

In Spanish, the preposition **a** and the masculine singular definite article **el** always contract into **al**. This is mandatory — you cannot write "a el". The contraction only applies to `a + el` (not `a + él`, the pronoun).

- `a + el = al` (always)
- `a + la` = stays as `a la` (no contraction)
- `a + los` = stays as `a los` (no contraction)

The contraction appears in several common contexts:

1. **Movement toward a place:** `ir al` (to go to the)
2. **Location phrases:** `al lado de` (beside), `al norte` (to the north), `al final de la calle` (at the end of the street)
3. **Infinitive constructions:** `al + infinitive` = "upon doing" / "when doing"

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| ir al + place | `ir al bar`, `ir al campo` | go to the bar / field |
| al lado de | `al lado de la tienda` | next to the shop |
| al norte / sur / este / oeste | `al norte de Madrid` | to the north of Madrid |
| al final de | `Al final de la calle` | at the end of the street |
| al + infinitive | `Al llegar, llamé` | Upon arriving, I called |
