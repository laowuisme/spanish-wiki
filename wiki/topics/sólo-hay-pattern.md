# sólo hay / menos gente — Quantifier Patterns

## Rule

### sólo / solo before hay

`Sólo hay un camarón.` — There is only one shrimp.

`sólo` (or `solo` — the RAE removed the accent rule in 2010 but both are accepted) placed before `hay` means "only / there is only." This word order is standard: the quantifier comes before the existential verb `hay`.

### menos gente / mucha gente

`menos gente` — fewer people (gente is feminine singular, even though it refers to multiple people)
`mucha gente` — many people (NOT `mucho gente` — must agree: `mucha` because gente is feminine)

### un par de + plural noun

`Un par de + noun` — a couple of / a pair of:
- `un par de horas` — a couple of hours
- `un par de zapatos` — a pair of shoes

`un par de` always takes a plural noun regardless of the number implied.

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| sólo hay | `Sólo hay una silla.` | There is only one chair. |
| menos/más + noun | `menos gente` | fewer people |
| mucha gente | `Hay mucha gente.` | There are many people. |
| un par de + noun | `un par de días` | a couple of days |

## Your Mistakes

- `mucho gente lo hace` — wrong. Correct: `mucha gente lo hace` (gente is feminine).
